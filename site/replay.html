    The simulated follow

    A point drone that turns and moves as the council commands, within placeholder limits of 10 m/s and 90 degrees a second. It is not a flight simulator: there is no physics, wind, autopilot or camera, and a return-home request is recorded, not flown.

    Replay your own

    A recording. Record a walk or ride with a GPS-logging app on your phone, export it as GPX, and open it above. This page runs the repo's own Python in this tab with Pyodide, about 13 MB to load the first time, and simulates a drone following you. The file is not uploaded, and the page keeps only meters and seconds from its first fix.

    A log. Make a trace with the replay tool, then open it above:

    PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m follow_me.replay --json LOG.json > trace.json

    Record only yourself, or a rider who has agreed. The log format is described at the top of src/follow_me/replay.py, and recordings in docs/simulation.md. Download the examples: the log, its trace, or the simulated follow. Python in the browser: Pyodide 314.0.7 (MPL-2.0), served unmodified from this site.

    How to read it

    • Attention scales a source's weight by conditions: rain lowers the camera, a weak link or fix lowers a companion, and a known trouble spot can switch a source off.
    • Trust is the reading's reliability after attention. Zero means the source is off.
    • The gate rejects a reading that sits more than about three sigma from the gate centre, a reliability-weighted median of the active readings.
    • The fused estimate combines the kept readings, weighting each by trust divided by variance.
    • Modes time a loss in seconds since the last estimate: dead reckoning for up to 5 s, then hold, and a return-home request after 50 s, however often the loop runs.
