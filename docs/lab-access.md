# Lab and club access (FAU engineering, IEEE)

The owner has access to 3D printers at FAU's engineering building and at the IEEE club. Other resources are unknown until someone asks. This doc lists what to ask, and what to prepare before asking.

## Known so far

- FAU's School of Architecture runs a FabLab in Fort Lauderdale with laser cutters, 3D printers, a CNC machine, a 3D scanner and a wood shop. A safety orientation comes first, and it enrolls users in the laser cutter, 3D printer and wood shop orientations.
- Ask whether any FabLab laser is a fiber laser. Most makerspace CO2 lasers cannot cut steel, so stator laminations need a fiber laser, a cutting service, or etching.
- No FAU cleanroom turned up in a search. The University of Florida's Nanoscale Research Facility has cleanrooms for UF-affiliated users.
- Group orders through the IEEE club can meet factory minimum orders (see [supply-side.md](supply-side.md)).

## Framing

This is a personal hobby and learning project, written by AI coding assistants at the owner's direction. It is not a company, a product, or a university project unless the lab or club decides to make it one. Describe it that way in any request.

## Printers (first priority)

Ask:

- Which printers are available: model, build volume, nozzle sizes, and whether a hardened nozzle is fitted.
- Which materials are allowed: PLA, PETG, ASA, nylon, carbon-fibre nylon.
- Whether the lab supplies filament or the owner brings their own.
- Whether printing flight parts (frames, prop guards, mounts) is allowed, and what testing the lab expects.
- Charges: per gram, per hour, or free. Print time limits and queueing.
- Training required, and who can print unsupervised.

## Electronics

Ask about:

- A CNC mill (3018 class or larger) for isolation milling, or a PCB etching setup.
- Soldering stations, hot-air or reflow equipment, and a microscope.
- Bench power supplies with current limits, an oscilloscope, a logic analyser, and multimeters.
- A GNSS receiver or antenna test setup, and a Wi-Fi test setup.
- Solder paste, flux, and PCB blanks.

## Mechanical and motor work

Ask about:

- A load cell, a scale that reads grams, and calipers.
- A laser cutter (for stator laminations or stencils) and a CNC router.
- Motor winding tools, or a space to build them.
- Magnet wire, N52 magnets, and bearings, bought or already in stock.

## Drone club

The owner reports access to a drone club (2026-10-26). It is not confirmed here: an agent web search on 2026-10-26 found no university-level FAU drone club, only the K-12 drone club at FAU's lab schools. Things worth asking, none of them money:

- Whether an experienced pilot would act as safety pilot for the later gates (tethered or caged, then free flight), and where the club flies legally.
- Whether a transmitter can be borrowed. It is the largest placeholder in the drone passes ($60 to $120).
- Whether crashed or retired drones can be salvaged for parts.
- Whether members would test the follow logic as riders who agree to it, or try the website and the replay viewer.
- Whether members want to join a group order. It is cost-sharing: each member pays for the parts they receive.

## Parts and tools, not money

The owner does not like asking for money. Nothing here asks for funding, and no agent should draft a funding, sponsorship or grant request unless the owner asks for one. Funding is optional and is not part of the plan.

Ask about:

- Whether surplus or old lab parts can be used.
- Whether tools can be booked or borrowed, and what training they need.
- Whether a group order can run through the club. It is cost-sharing: each member pays for the parts they receive.

Nothing in this repo has been confirmed with FAU or IEEE.

## Rules to check before any flight

- FAU's environmental health and safety drone page (undated, found by an agent web search on 2026-10-26) says recreational drone use on university property is strictly prohibited, and that other flights need approval through FAU Risk Management, pending a formal university policy. Printing parts at FAU does not make flying there allowed. Confirm the current rule with FAU before any flight on university property.
- FAU's Boca Raton campus borders Boca Raton Airport, which has Class D airspace. Flying near it needs FAA authorization, which LAANC gives through approved apps, including to recreational flyers. Check the FAA's B4UFLY app or UAS facility map before any flight.
- Check local and federal drone rules. The privacy and rules doc lists what to verify.
- Lithium battery charging and storage rules for the lab.
- After-hours access and insurance, if any.
